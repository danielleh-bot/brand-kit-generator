Here's where World Cup heat could be the biggest concern

With temperatures expected to soar above 100°F during the 2026 FIFA World Cup, athletes and fans heading to certain host cities face serious heat-related risks.

By Jonathan Erdman and Chris Dolce June 2, 2026

The 2026 FIFA World Cup is expected to be the largest sporting event ever held, with matches spread across 16 cities in the United States, Mexico, and Canada this summer. But for several of those host cities, extreme heat will be a major factor for players and the millions of fans attending matches.

Dallas, Houston, and several Mexican host cities — including Guadalajara and Monterrey — are forecast to see temperatures well above 100°F during peak tournament days in late June and July. Even with evening kickoff times, heat index values could remain dangerously high.

FIFA has built cooling breaks and hydration protocols into match schedules, but sports medicine experts warn that the combination of humidity, sustained physical exertion, and packed stadiums creates compounding risk. 'This isn't just about the athletes,' said Dr. Sarah Chen, a sports medicine researcher at UT Southwestern. 'Fan safety in and around stadiums is equally critical.'

Northern venues like Seattle, Vancouver, and Toronto will offer cooler conditions, but organizers aren't taking any chances — mobile cooling stations, misting fans, and extended shade structures are being deployed across all 16 host cities.

The Weather Channel will provide city-by-city forecasts and heat safety guidance throughout the tournament, which kicks off June 11 and runs through July 19.
