SpaceX's $60 billion bet on Cursor just pulled its 25-year-old CEO into Elon Musk's orbit — and aimed him straight at Anthropic

Michael Truell dropped out of MIT and was interning at Google at 18. Now he runs the AI coding company Elon Musk wants most — and insiders say the real target is Anthropic's Claude Code.

By Marcus Reilly and Priya Nandakumar Updated Jun. 12, 2026, 7:42 AM ET

When SpaceX disclosed that it had secured the right to buy Cursor for $60 billion later this year, the deal instantly made the AI coding startup one of the most valuable private companies in the world — and turned its 25-year-old chief executive, Michael Truell, into Silicon Valley's newest billionaire founder.

Truell enrolled at MIT, finished a single year, and left to build what would become Cursor with three classmates. At 18 he had already interned at Google, working on the large language models that ranked content in users' feeds. Forbes now pegs his net worth at roughly $1.3 billion.

The structure of the agreement is unusual: if Elon Musk's rocket company declines to exercise its option, it will still pay Cursor $10 billion for the work the two companies do together — a floor that all but guarantees the startup a windfall either way.

People close to the talks say the tie-up is really about catching Anthropic. The AI lab's Claude Code product helped push its annualized revenue past $30 billion as of April, and xAI has fallen well behind both Claude Code and OpenAI's Codex in developer tools.

"This isn't a rocket story, it's a coding story," said one investor briefed on the deal. "Whoever owns the tool developers live in all day owns the next platform. Musk knows he's behind in that race, and Cursor is the fastest way back into it."
