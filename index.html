The ultrawealthy are spending $150,000 a year on 'lifestyle managers' to plan their leisure and social calendars

A new tier of concierge service has emerged beyond traditional wealth management — personal executives who orchestrate every detail of life away from the boardroom.

By Juliet Bennett Rylah Jun. 6, 2026, 9:30 AM ET

They are not assistants in any traditional sense. They don't manage calendars for work meetings or coordinate travel for business trips. What a growing cohort of firms is selling to clients worth $10 million or more is something more intimate — and, according to those who offer it, more essential: the complete orchestration of a wealthy person's personal life.

These "lifestyle managers" — a term used loosely across the industry — book the right table at a Michelin three-star on a Tuesday night two weeks out, source vintage Burgundy for a client's anniversary dinner, coordinate the social invitations for a black-tie charity gala, and ensure that the family ski trip to Val d'Isère does not overlap with a school recital. The annual retainer, for what practitioners describe as a "chief of staff for your personal life," typically runs between $80,000 and $150,000.

"Our clients have already optimized everything at work. What they want is for someone to do the same for the rest of their lives — the art, the table, the weekend, the birthday — so they can simply show up." — Founder, a lifestyle management firm catering to New York and Miami clients

The category has grown quietly but rapidly since 2020, buoyed by record wealth creation at the top of the income scale and a post-pandemic recalibration of priorities among the ultra-high-net-worth set. At least a dozen firms now operate in this specific niche, up from two or three a decade ago, according to industry observers. Several have waiting lists.

What distinguishes the category from older luxury concierge services — white-glove hotel programs, private banking amenities, or American Express Centurion — is the depth of the relationship and the exclusivity of the access. "We have 14 clients," says the founder of one New York-based firm. "That's intentional. You can't do this for 400 people."

The services are not merely logistical. Several practitioners interviewed for this story described an almost therapeutic dimension: helping clients articulate what they actually want from their free time, not just execute on it. One manager said she spends two to three hours in onboarding conversations simply mapping out a new client's social and cultural life — their museum preferences, which friendships they want to invest in, which charity commitments feel obligatory versus meaningful.

Pricing varies. At the lower end, curated calendar and event access runs $80,000 annually. Full-service lifestyle management — what one operator described as "a second chief of staff, but for your whole life" — is typically $120,000 to $175,000, with some bespoke arrangements exceeding that figure for clients who require a dedicated on-call manager rather than a shared one.
